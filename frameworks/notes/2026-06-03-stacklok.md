# Stacklok

Date: 2026-06-03

## Why I Looked At It

While exploring agent infrastructure, I realized my Coordination Layer Hypothesis was missing something.

Coordination is important.

But coordination without trust is dangerous.

Stacklok appears focused on the trust layer around agents.

---

## What Stood Out

Focus areas include:

* Agent security
* Identity
* Permissions
* Governance
* MCP infrastructure
* Policy enforcement
* Auditability

The company seems less interested in making agents smarter and more interested in making agents safe.

---

## Observation

As agents become more capable, organizations need answers to questions such as:

* Who owns this agent?
* What can it access?
* What actions can it perform?
* Who approved those actions?
* How are actions audited?
* What happens when the agent makes a mistake?

These are not intelligence problems.

They are trust problems.

---

## Connection to Metadata

The phrase "treat agents like humans" keeps coming back.

Humans operate within systems of metadata:

* Identity
* Role
* Permissions
* Team
* Manager
* Budget
* Performance metrics

Agents may require similar metadata.

Without metadata:

* Agents are difficult to govern.

With metadata:

* Agents become manageable participants in a larger system.

---

## Framework Update

Original thinking:

Resources
→ Intelligence
→ Coordination
→ Execution
→ Feedback
→ Optimization

Updated thinking:

Trust, Security, Identity, Governance, and Observability are cross-cutting concerns that influence every stage.

They are not separate steps.

They are properties of the system.

---

## Potential Insight

As intelligence becomes abundant:

Coordination becomes more important.

As coordination becomes automated:

Trust becomes more important.

---

## Open Questions

* What metadata should every agent have?
* How should agent identity work?
* What is the equivalent of least privilege for agents?
* How should agent actions be audited?
* Does trust become the next bottleneck after coordination?
