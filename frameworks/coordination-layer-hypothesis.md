# Coordination Layer Hypothesis

Version: 0.1

Author: Kelly Sable

## Core Thesis

As AI intelligence becomes abundant and commoditized, value shifts to the coordination layer around intelligence.

The primary challenge is no longer generating intelligence.

The primary challenge becomes coordinating intelligence.

Historically, organizations competed on access to information.

Increasingly, organizations will compete on how effectively they coordinate humans, AI systems, agents, workflows, and decisions.

---

## The Evolution of Scarcity

### Industrial Era

Scarcity:

* Physical labor

Organizations optimized:

* Factories
* Machines
* Supply chains

### Information Era

Scarcity:

* Information

Organizations optimized:

* Databases
* Search
* Networks
* Cloud platforms

### AI Era

Emerging scarcity:

* Coordination

Information is increasingly abundant.

Intelligence is increasingly abundant.

The bottleneck shifts toward:

* Prioritization
* Governance
* Routing
* Trust
* Ownership
* Workflow design
* Security
* Context management

## Framework

Resources
→ Intelligence
→ Coordination
→ Execution
→ Feedback
→ Optimization

### Resources

Inputs available to a system.

Examples:

* People
* Money
* Compute
* Data
* Time
* Attention

### Intelligence

The ability to generate:

* Information
* Analysis
* Decisions
* Recommendations
* Code
* Content

Examples:

* Humans
* LLMs
* Agents
* Search systems

### Coordination

The systems that determine how intelligence is used.

Examples:

* Governance
* Workflow orchestration
* Routing
* Ownership
* Prioritization
* Memory systems
* Context management
* Communication

This is the layer I believe is becoming increasingly important.

### Execution

Where work occurs.

Examples:

* Software development
* Content creation
* Customer support
* Manufacturing
* Decision making

### Feedback

Signals produced by execution.

Examples:

* Telemetry
* Cost
* Outcomes
* Customer response
* Incidents

### Optimization

Improving future coordination based on feedback.

## Metadata Principle

Intelligence does not scale by itself.

Coordination requires metadata.

As AI systems become more capable, organizations need mechanisms to identify, govern, route, observe, trust, and manage intelligence.

Metadata enables coordination.

Without metadata:

* Intelligence is isolated.

With metadata:

* Intelligence becomes manageable.

* Escalation paths

A person is not simply intelligence.

## Working Summary

AI does not primarily reduce the cost of labor.

AI does not primarily reduce the cost of information.

AI reduces the cost of intelligence.

As intelligence becomes cheaper and more abundant, coordination becomes increasingly valuable.

The future competitive advantage may come less from generating intelligence and more from coordinating intelligence effectively.
